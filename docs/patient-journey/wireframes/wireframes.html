Section 1

How to read this document

These are wireframes, not final designs. They show what goes on each screen and in what order: the content, the buttons and the steps. Colours, photos, fonts and final wording come later. Grey boxes with a cross are placeholders for images.

Prices and names are examples. Prices come from the current website and will be confirmed for each country. "Clinician name" is a placeholder.

Numbered black dots on a screen match the notes beside it. Each note says what that part does, and whether it is new or changed.

Solid frame: this screen exists on today's website
Dashed frame: a new screen we are proposing
ChangedSomething that works differently from today
NewSomething that doesn't exist today

Section 3

What we changed, and why

Compared with today's website and the original plan. Each change has the same aim: fewer surprises, and fewer steps between "I need help" and "I'm talking to a doctor".

#Before (today's website)After (this design)Why it's better
1The service details window says "Explore on Carevia". It opens the general services page, so the patient has to choose again.The button says "Book General care" (or whichever service) and opens booking with that service already chosen.One less decision. The patient doesn't lose their place.
2"Find your care", "Browse our care" and the footer button all scroll down to the same list. No button starts a booking.Browsing still scrolls to the list. Booking starts from any service's details. Returning patients get "Book new care" in their account.Clear difference between "look around" and "book now".
3The website has "Create your account" but no way to sign in."Sign in" sits in the top bar. Returning patients go straight to their account home (screen 12).Returning patients don't have to start from the homepage.
4The 18+ rule and the country limit are only in the FAQ answers.Booking step 1 asks "Who is this visit for?" and "Where will you be?" before anything else.Nobody finds out they can't book after giving their details or card.
5"Call 112" is in the FAQ and the service details note.It is also on booking step 1 and in the waiting room.The message appears where someone in a hurry will see it.
6No booking flow existed, so the original plan had no rule for when to create the account.The patient chooses a clinician and time first. The account comes after, and the time is held for 10 minutes.People see real availability before being asked for personal details. More people finish booking.

Section 3, continued

What we changed, and why

#Before (today's website)After (this design)Why it's better
7"Pay per visit, no subscription" and "free rebook if the video fails" are in the FAQ and the "Your first visit" section.Both are repeated on the payment screen, right beside the Pay button.Reassurance appears at the moment of doubt.
8Preparation tips are written as a short article.The tips become a checklist on the confirmation screen, with a "Test camera and mic" button.Easier to act on. Technical problems are caught before the call, not during it.
9Nothing between booking and the call.A waiting room shows camera, mic and connection status, and offers a free rebook if something is wrong.Calms the most anxious moment.
10Patients can't choose a time and a clinician on the website.A "First available" option, plus filters for day and language.The fastest route for urgent needs, and choice for people who want it.
11"Your care in one place" was a promise only.A summary page (screen 11) and an account home (screen 12) with visits, documents and family members.Everything after the call is in one place, as promised.
12Children and dependants are mentioned only in the FAQ."Me / My child / Another adult I care for" in booking step 1, and a Family section in the account.Parents can book for a child without a workaround.
13Seven service cards. A patient who isn't sure which fits has no way in.An eighth card: "Not sure which one?", which starts with General care.Undecided patients still have an easy first step.

Web compared with mobile

Booking summary on the side

On web, your service, time and price stay visible on the right through every booking step. On mobile they appear on the review screen.

Service details as a window

Web shows details in a centred window with a photo. Mobile slides up a panel from the bottom.

Your questions beside the video

On web, the questions you wrote before the call sit beside the video. Mobile keeps the screen for the call only.

Account menu on the left

Web has a left menu (Home, Visits, Documents, Family). Mobile uses a compact menu at the top.

Section 6

When things don't go to plan

A good journey also handles the bad days. These four situations are designed into the screens above.

It's an emergency

Where: any screen.

"Call 112" shows on the service details, the search (for urgent-sounding words), booking step 1, the waiting room and the summary. Carevia never looks like an emergency service.

I can't use Carevia (country or age)

Where: booking step 1 (screen 04).

Checked before any personal details or payment. The patient sees a short, kind explanation and what to do instead. Under-18s are booked by a parent or guardian.

The video doesn't work

Where: waiting room or during the call (screens 09–10).

One tap to rebook for free. Booking reopens at step 2 with the service and patient already filled in, so only a new time is needed.

It can't be treated online

Where: during the call, then the summary (screen 11).

The clinician recommends in-person care. The summary shows a referral and clear next steps instead of a prescription.

Section 7

Questions for you, and next steps

Your answers will shape the next round of design. None of these block the journey above, but each changes a screen.

Questions

1. Choosing a clinician. Should patients pick a specific clinician, or only a time ("first available")? We show both. Screen 05

2. Holding a time. Is 10 minutes right while a new patient creates an account? Screen 06

3. Payment. Which payment provider? Do we need insurance, vouchers or company billing? Screen 07

4. Cancelling. Until when can a patient cancel or reschedule for free? Screens 08, 12

5. Countries and languages. Which countries and languages at launch? Screens 04, 05

6. Emergency wording. Can your clinical team approve the emergency messages and the symptom words that trigger them? Screens 01b, 04, 09

7. Recordings. Are calls recorded? The website currently says to ask support. Screen 10

Suggested next steps

1. Review this document

Mark anything to add, remove or reorder. Answer the questions on the left.

2. Mid-fidelity designs

Real wording, Carevia's colours and type, for the booking steps first (screens 03–07), since that's where people drop off.

3. Clickable prototype

Test it with five real patients before anything is built.
